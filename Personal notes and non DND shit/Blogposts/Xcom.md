Ive been thinking about the things that make Xcom 2 tick, and why the meta progression feels as good as it does. 

**Gametime** dont lose
One of the fundamental feelings that drives this game is anxiety the players larger conflict with ADVENT. When I first picked up this game I was shocked by how stressed I was on a minute to minute level. Every character death had bite and the games lethality made it very clear very quickly that that was a legitimate risk the player would have to take seriously.

Crunchy somewhat arcane systems run in the background. They promise depth and weighty decisions.  

This however has a cost, If one were to bring these elements to table top there are significant tradeoffs them would have to make. 

LETHALITY and RISK
- To begin, no GM is a computer (if you are I love you BTW) and running systems Like this would be massively labor intensive, time consuming, and error prone. Absolutely no one wants to loose a character or two because these a detail of one of these systems inevitably fell through the cracks. 
- Secondly if losing matters consensus matters so much more, getting a table of players to make a conclusive decision is difficult and increases exponentially as the number of possibilities goes up. It is important to limit player choice in these scenarios 
- Repetition, as with any skill repetition is central to ones ability to better themselves. Adults have a difficult time getting to the table 
- Chance is not drama, its risk and its difficult for a player to process risk when there's a chance to miss three clear shots in a row and receive a party wipe for it. With a game that embraces permadeath, unless there are guard rails for being very unlucky, speed is an important element to player satisfaction. A player needs to move on and the best way to get that done is to shift focus, there a new mission to complete, a new character to level, the game is larger than that party wipe. However tabletop by its analog nature is slow and laborious. 

It may be helpful to look at how a game like mothership uses this risk level. Mothership is extremely lethal and it deals with many of these problems by shifting its attitude toward combat and genre from action to horror. While many of its rules are devoted to combat, the designer chose to limit skill depth, meaning a character can buy better armor and gain important skills but they are just as likely to die with twenty five sessions under their belt as they are with two and the gm is not wasting their time thinking of how high level abilities interact with a given scene. Perhaps more importantly, the unfairness of the combat is explicit, players do not have to reach consensus on wining a combat because the objective is to escape or prevent a fight to begin with.

Games like spire and heart focus on the narrative side of this as well. In these systems not only is that loss or death preordained but it fuels progression. Its combat is far less lethal but because the games objective is struggle, it can focus on both organizations and individuals in a way that is narratively satisfying

So how do we make a game that is invested in fighting, how do we emphasize loss while making battles winnable.
