# Cells within cells
The otherness or anxiety posed by a hive mind is of course about a loss of individuality, a lack of personhood or soul necessitated by their level of functionality, their service to a single collective purpose. 

We must note that this fear is similar, but ultimately unlike the loss of individuality we see in zombies or other tropes that concern "stollen bodies." Zombies specifically target the "loss" of a soul, something that looks like you, functionally IS you, but with everything that matters stripped away. (Realistically zombies are about a lot more than this but go with me )

Hive minds also touch on what we fear from aliens and computers. Things with sentience, motivations and significant means, clear personhood, but a personhood not bound by any human conventions and often in direct conflict with them. 

Because hive minds imply the individuality of a single body, physically distinct from the collective, regardless of the form that body takes, it presents a shape that the reader, watcher, player fills in. When we meet a member of a hive mind our conceptions of a character cant help but impose individuality onto it. I personally don't think that this is a bad impulse, we are human and its not at all that useful to pretend out interpretations wont be touched by that. If anything we can use it, that soul players fill in makes them feel something, and when the ant person they've been traveling with doesn't understand the concept of a nickname or kills without mercy and a utilitarian perspective, that feeling might motivate them to do something. 

in short: there is tension between the human players idea of personhood and an individual of a hive mind's inhuman nature, when playing with hive mind fiction it is the GMs responsibility to facilitate that tension
