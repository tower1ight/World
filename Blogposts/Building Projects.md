# The Importance of Public Works 
Everyone is quick to say cities are living and breathing things and change is important to illustrate that life, its somewhat simple to drop in npc's feelings about recent shifts or movements in political power, or a oncoming threat to the cities populous. but often, at least in my experience, this can feel rather flat and leave the players mind as soon as possible. A location on a map and a short description of how it looks in the skyline can go far. 

Large iconic buildings are great ways to represent a cities history, culturally, politically, economically, in the same way new or in progress building projects are tools for communicating the present and future of that city. Plus who doesn't love a sword fight on the fantasy equivalent of an I beam. 
### Bridges and Roads
Bridges and roads *wistful sigh*...  visually separated from other surrounding structures, they take foreeeeeever to build, and the connection of two sides is a great flexible metaphor for whatever is going on in your city. the progress represented by two separate places becoming one, scientific developments manifesting in new structures that couldn't be built before, brewing conflict at a barrier about to drop, you name it, bridges and roads can emphasize whatever tension is going on

Bridges are visually striking, what is it evoking

### Slum Clearing
These locations require more care and attention and are more sensitive to genre, they are less applicable as "set dressing" than other items on this list. But can add great texture and gravity to class disparity present in your game.

From a writing perspective slum clearing is less about "why is this happening?" and "who is causing them?" I certainly understand the impulse to establish cause and effect, but in many ways a "reason" can serve as justification and distract from the weight of the situation. See the problem with players "solving" colonization or other oppressive systems decolonizing {citation}. 

What is more important in this case are they people cast out. 
Who are they?
- Neighborhoods have Identity 
Where are they? 
- was other "housing" arranged for them?
- Are some of them trying to reform their community and keep neighborly ties in another district? How successful has that been?
How are other neighborhoods dealing with an influx of new people? 
- How do those opinions play with the opinions about whatever is being built in its place
- Are these opinons shared by all? If so who is influencing them
Who is taking advantage of their desperation?
